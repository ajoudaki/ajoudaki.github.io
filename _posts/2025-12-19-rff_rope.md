<h1 id="introduction">Introduction</h1>
<p>Self-attention is permutation-equivariant, hence Transformers require
positional information to represent ordered or structured inputs <span
class="citation" data-cites="vaswani2017attention">(<a
href="#ref-vaswani2017attention" role="doc-biblioref">Vaswani et al.
2017</a>)</span>. Rotary Position Embedding (RoPE) <span
class="citation" data-cites="su2021roformer">(<a
href="#ref-su2021roformer" role="doc-biblioref">Su et al.
2021</a>)</span> injects relative position information by applying
position-dependent <span class="math inline">\(2\times 2\)</span>
rotations to query/key feature pairs. In its common deterministic form,
RoPE uses a fixed geometric progression of frequencies (angles) <span
class="citation" data-cites="su2021roformer">(<a
href="#ref-su2021roformer" role="doc-biblioref">Su et al.
2021</a>)</span>, which couples the induced positional interactions to a
particular spectral profile and limits the ability to encode
domain-specific inductive biases. Extensions and adaptations of RoPE to
higher-dimensional structure (e.g., 2D vision) further motivate a
principled design view <span class="citation"
data-cites="li2021learnablefourier heo2024ropevit">(<a
href="#ref-li2021learnablefourier" role="doc-biblioref">Li et al.
2021</a>; <a href="#ref-heo2024ropevit" role="doc-biblioref">Heo et al.
2024</a>)</span>.</p>
<p>We introduce <em>Random Feature RoPE (RF-RoPE)</em>: rather than
fixing frequencies, we <em>sample</em> them from a designed distribution
<span class="math inline">\(p(\boldsymbol{\omega})\)</span>. Our central
result is a kernel-design theorem showing that RF-RoPE implements any
desired shift-invariant kernel in expectation at initialization. This
turns positional-encoding design into a mathematically grounded inverse
problem: <span class="math display">\[\Phi(\Delta \mathbf{p})
\;\longrightarrow\; p(\boldsymbol{\omega}) \;\longrightarrow\;
\{\boldsymbol{\omega}_i\}_{i=1}^{D},\]</span> with the inverse map
justified by Bochner’s theorem <span class="citation"
data-cites="bochner1955harmonic rasmussen2006gpml">(<a
href="#ref-bochner1955harmonic" role="doc-biblioref">Bochner 1955</a>;
<a href="#ref-rasmussen2006gpml" role="doc-biblioref">Rasmussen and
Williams 2006</a>)</span> and operationalized via random features <span
class="citation" data-cites="rahimi2007random">(<a
href="#ref-rahimi2007random" role="doc-biblioref">Rahimi and Recht
2007</a>)</span>.</p>
<h1 id="background-and-related-work">Background and Related Work</h1>
<h4 id="random-fourier-features-and-bochner.">Random Fourier Features
and Bochner.</h4>
<p>Random Fourier Features (RFF) approximate shift-invariant kernels via
Monte Carlo samples from their spectral density <span class="citation"
data-cites="rahimi2007random">(<a href="#ref-rahimi2007random"
role="doc-biblioref">Rahimi and Recht 2007</a>)</span>. Bochner’s
theorem characterizes such kernels as Fourier transforms of nonnegative
measures <span class="citation"
data-cites="bochner1955harmonic rasmussen2006gpml">(<a
href="#ref-bochner1955harmonic" role="doc-biblioref">Bochner 1955</a>;
<a href="#ref-rasmussen2006gpml" role="doc-biblioref">Rasmussen and
Williams 2006</a>)</span>.</p>
<h4
id="random-features-in-efficient-attention-and-positional-encoding.">Random
features in efficient attention and positional encoding.</h4>
<p>Random features have been used to approximate attention kernels in
linear-time Transformers (e.g., Performer) <span class="citation"
data-cites="choromanski2021performer">(<a
href="#ref-choromanski2021performer" role="doc-biblioref">Choromanski et
al. 2021</a>)</span>. Separately, Stochastic Positional Encoding (SPE)
generates relative positional behavior compatible with linear attention
<span class="citation" data-cites="liutkus2021relative">(<a
href="#ref-liutkus2021relative" role="doc-biblioref">Liutkus et al.
2021</a>)</span>, and F-StrIPE extends random-feature positional
encodings with structure-informed priors in symbolic music <span
class="citation" data-cites="agarwal2025fstripe">(<a
href="#ref-agarwal2025fstripe" role="doc-biblioref">Agarwal et al.
2025</a>)</span>. In parallel, RoPE variants have explored learned or
content-dependent rotations (e.g., Selective RoPE) <span
class="citation" data-cites="movahedi2025selectiverope">(<a
href="#ref-movahedi2025selectiverope" role="doc-biblioref">Movahedi et
al. 2025</a>)</span>.</p>
<h4 id="novelty-gap.">Novelty gap.</h4>
<p>Prior random-feature PE work primarily builds additive feature maps
for (often linear) attention. Our contribution is to identify the
<em>RoPE rotation operator itself</em> as an RFF-style kernel estimator
when frequencies are sampled symmetrically. This yields a direct,
efficient pathway to import kernel-design theory into the RoPE
mechanism.</p>
<h1 id="the-rf-rope-framework">The RF-RoPE Framework</h1>
<h2
id="generalized-rotational-encoding-for-mathbfpinmathbbrk">Generalized
rotational encoding for <span
class="math inline">\(\mathbf{p}\in\mathbb{R}^k\)</span></h2>
<p>Let <span
class="math inline">\(\mathbf{q}_m,\mathbf{k}_n\in\mathbb{R}^d\)</span>
be query/key vectors at positions <span
class="math inline">\(\mathbf{p}_m,\mathbf{p}_n\in\mathbb{R}^k\)</span>.
Assume <span class="math inline">\(d\)</span> is even and split features
into <span class="math inline">\(D=d/2\)</span> two-dimensional blocks:
<span class="math display">\[\mathbf{q}_m =
(\mathbf{q}_{m,1},\ldots,\mathbf{q}_{m,D}),\qquad
\mathbf{k}_n = (\mathbf{k}_{n,1},\ldots,\mathbf{k}_{n,D}),\]</span>
where each <span
class="math inline">\(\mathbf{q}_{m,i},\mathbf{k}_{n,i}\in\mathbb{R}^2\)</span>.</p>
<p>For each block <span class="math inline">\(i\)</span>, sample an
i.i.d. frequency vector <span
class="math inline">\(\boldsymbol{\omega}_i\in\mathbb{R}^k\)</span> from
<span class="math inline">\(p(\boldsymbol{\omega})\)</span>. Define the
<span class="math inline">\(2\times 2\)</span> rotation <span
class="math display">\[R(\alpha) \;=\;
\begin{pmatrix}
\cos\alpha &amp; -\sin\alpha\\
\sin\alpha &amp; \cos\alpha
\end{pmatrix}.\]</span> RF-RoPE applies the phase <span
class="math inline">\(\alpha=\mathbf{p}\cdot\boldsymbol{\omega}_i\)</span>:
<span class="math display">\[\mathbf{q}&#39;_{m,i} =
R(\mathbf{p}_m\cdot\boldsymbol{\omega}_i)\,\mathbf{q}_{m,i},
\qquad
\mathbf{k}&#39;_{n,i} =
R(\mathbf{p}_n\cdot\boldsymbol{\omega}_i)\,\mathbf{k}_{n,i},\]</span>
and concatenates <span
class="math inline">\(\mathbf{q}&#39;_m=(\mathbf{q}&#39;_{m,1},\dots,\mathbf{q}&#39;_{m,D})\)</span>,
<span
class="math inline">\(\mathbf{k}&#39;_n=(\mathbf{k}&#39;_{n,1},\dots,\mathbf{k}&#39;_{n,D})\)</span>.</p>
<h2 id="per-block-inner-product-decomposition">Per-block inner product
decomposition</h2>
<p>Let <span
class="math inline">\(\Delta\mathbf{p}=\mathbf{p}_n-\mathbf{p}_m\)</span>
and <span
class="math inline">\(u_i=\Delta\mathbf{p}\cdot\boldsymbol{\omega}_i\)</span>.
Using the rotation identity <span class="math inline">\(R(a)^\top
R(b)=R(b-a)\)</span>, the contribution of block <span
class="math inline">\(i\)</span> to the RoPE-modulated dot product is
<span class="math display">\[\begin{aligned}
S_i
\;:=\;
(\mathbf{q}&#39;_{m,i})^\top \mathbf{k}&#39;_{n,i}
&amp;=
\mathbf{q}_{m,i}^\top R(u_i)\,\mathbf{k}_{n,i}.
\end{aligned}\]</span> Introduce the <span
class="math inline">\(+90^\circ\)</span> rotation matrix <span
class="math inline">\(J=
\begin{pmatrix}
0 &amp; -1\\
1 &amp; 0
\end{pmatrix}\)</span> so that <span
class="math inline">\(J\mathbf{x}\)</span> rotates <span
class="math inline">\(\mathbf{x}\)</span> by <span
class="math inline">\(+90^\circ\)</span>. Then <span
class="math display">\[R(u) = \cos u \,I_2 + \sin u \,J.\]</span>
Substituting yields the exact decomposition <span
class="math display">\begin{equation}\label{eq:Si-decomp}
S_i
=
(\mathbf{q}_{m,i}^\top \mathbf{k}_{n,i})\cos u_i
+
(\mathbf{q}_{m,i}^\top J\mathbf{k}_{n,i})\sin u_i.\end{equation}</span> The full
RF-RoPE attention score (pre-softmax) is <span
class="math display">\[S(\mathbf{p}_m,\mathbf{p}_n)
:= (\mathbf{q}&#39;_m)^\top \mathbf{k}&#39;_n
= \sum_{i=1}^{D} S_i.\]</span></p>
<h2 id="master-theorem-kernelization-in-expectation">Master Theorem:
kernelization in expectation</h2>
<div class="lemma">
<p><strong>Lemma 1</strong> (Odd-term cancellation under central
symmetry). <em> Fix <span
class="math inline">\(\Delta\mathbf{p}\in\mathbb{R}^k\)</span> and let
<span
class="math inline">\(u=\Delta\mathbf{p}\cdot\boldsymbol{\omega}\)</span>
with <span class="math inline">\(\boldsymbol{\omega}\sim
p(\boldsymbol{\omega})\)</span>. If <span
class="math inline">\(p\)</span> is centrally symmetric, i.e. <span
class="math inline">\(p(\boldsymbol{\omega})=p(-\boldsymbol{\omega})\)</span>,
then <span class="math display">\[\mathbb{E}[\sin u]=0,
\qquad
\mathbb{E}[\sin u \cos u]=0.\]</span></em></p>
</div>
<div class="proof">
<p><em>Proof.</em> By symmetry and the change of variables <span
class="math inline">\(\boldsymbol{\omega}\mapsto
-\boldsymbol{\omega}\)</span>, <span
class="math display">\[\mathbb{E}[\sin(\Delta\mathbf{p}\cdot\boldsymbol{\omega})]
=
\int_{\mathbb{R}^k}\sin(\Delta\mathbf{p}\cdot\boldsymbol{\omega})\,p(\boldsymbol{\omega})\,d\boldsymbol{\omega}
=
\int_{\mathbb{R}^k}\sin(\Delta\mathbf{p}\cdot(-\boldsymbol{\omega}))\,p(-\boldsymbol{\omega})\,d\boldsymbol{\omega}
=
-\mathbb{E}[\sin(\Delta\mathbf{p}\cdot\boldsymbol{\omega})],\]</span>
hence it must be <span class="math inline">\(0\)</span>. The second
identity follows since <span class="math inline">\(\sin u\cos
u=\tfrac{1}{2}\sin(2u)\)</span> is also odd in <span
class="math inline">\(u\)</span>. ◻</p>
</div>
<p>Define the <em>RF-RoPE positional kernel</em> induced by <span
class="math inline">\(p\)</span> as the cosine transform <span
class="math display">\begin{equation}\label{eq:Phi-def}
\Phi(\Delta\mathbf{p})
:= \mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{\omega}\sim
p}\!\left[\cos(\Delta\mathbf{p}\cdot\boldsymbol{\omega})\right]
=
\int_{\mathbb{R}^k}\cos(\Delta\mathbf{p}\cdot\boldsymbol{\omega})\,p(\boldsymbol{\omega})\,d\boldsymbol{\omega}.\end{equation}</span></p>
<div class="theorem">
<p><strong>Theorem 2</strong> (Master Theorem (RF-RoPE kernelization)).
<em> Assume <span
class="math inline">\(\boldsymbol{\omega}_1,\ldots,\boldsymbol{\omega}_D\)</span>
are i.i.d. from a centrally symmetric distribution <span
class="math inline">\(p(\boldsymbol{\omega})\)</span> and RF-RoPE is
applied as above. Then for any fixed content vectors <span
class="math inline">\(\mathbf{q}_m,\mathbf{k}_n\)</span> and positions
<span class="math inline">\(\mathbf{p}_m,\mathbf{p}_n\)</span>, <span
class="math display">\[\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{\omega}_{1:D}}\!\left[S(\mathbf{p}_m,\mathbf{p}_n)\right]
=
(\mathbf{q}_m^\top
\mathbf{k}_n)\,\Phi(\mathbf{p}_n-\mathbf{p}_m),\]</span> where <span
class="math inline">\(\Phi\)</span> is given by <span
class="math inline">\(\eqref{eq:Phi-def}\)</span>.</em></p>
</div>
<div class="proof">
<p><em>Proof.</em> From <span
class="math inline">\(\eqref{eq:Si-decomp}\)</span> and <span
class="math inline">\(u_i=\Delta\mathbf{p}\cdot\boldsymbol{\omega}_i\)</span>,
<span class="math display">\[S_i
=
A_i\cos u_i + B_i\sin u_i,
\qquad
A_i:=\mathbf{q}_{m,i}^\top \mathbf{k}_{n,i},\;\;
B_i:=\mathbf{q}_{m,i}^\top J\mathbf{k}_{n,i}.\]</span> Taking
expectation over <span
class="math inline">\(\boldsymbol{\omega}_i\)</span> and applying
Lemma <span class="math inline">\(1\)</span>, <span
class="math display">\[\mathbb{E}[S_i]
=
A_i\,\mathbb{E}[\cos(\Delta\mathbf{p}\cdot\boldsymbol{\omega})]
+
B_i\,\mathbb{E}[\sin(\Delta\mathbf{p}\cdot\boldsymbol{\omega})]
=
A_i\,\Phi(\Delta\mathbf{p}).\]</span> Summing over blocks and using
linearity of expectation, <span
class="math display">\[\mathbb{E}\!\left[S(\mathbf{p}_m,\mathbf{p}_n)\right]
=
\sum_{i=1}^{D}\mathbb{E}[S_i]
=
\Phi(\Delta\mathbf{p})\sum_{i=1}^{D}A_i
=
\Phi(\Delta\mathbf{p})\,\mathbf{q}_m^\top \mathbf{k}_n,\]</span> since
<span class="math inline">\(\mathbf{q}_m^\top \mathbf{k}_n=\sum_{i=1}^D
\mathbf{q}_{m,i}^\top \mathbf{k}_{n,i}=\sum_{i=1}^D A_i\)</span>. ◻</p>
</div>
<div class="corollary">
<p><strong>Corollary 3</strong> (Inverse kernel design via Bochner).
<em> Let <span
class="math inline">\(\Phi:\mathbb{R}^k\to\mathbb{R}\)</span> be
continuous, shift-invariant, and positive definite with <span
class="math inline">\(\Phi(\mathbf{0})=1\)</span>. Then there exists a
centrally symmetric probability measure <span
class="math inline">\(p(\boldsymbol{\omega})\)</span> such that <span
class="math inline">\(\eqref{eq:Phi-def}\)</span> holds. Consequently,
RF-RoPE can realize <span class="math inline">\(\Phi\)</span> in
expectation at initialization by sampling <span
class="math inline">\(\boldsymbol{\omega}_i\sim p\)</span> and applying
Theorem <span class="math inline">\(2\)</span>.</em></p>
</div>
<div class="proof">
<p><em>Proof.</em> By Bochner’s theorem <span class="citation"
data-cites="bochner1955harmonic rasmussen2006gpml">(<a
href="#ref-bochner1955harmonic" role="doc-biblioref">Bochner 1955</a>;
<a href="#ref-rasmussen2006gpml" role="doc-biblioref">Rasmussen and
Williams 2006</a>)</span>, <span class="math inline">\(\Phi\)</span>
admits a representation <span
class="math display">\[\Phi(\Delta\mathbf{p})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^k}
e^{i\,\Delta\mathbf{p}^\top
\boldsymbol{\omega}}\,d\mu(\boldsymbol{\omega})\]</span> for a finite
nonnegative measure <span class="math inline">\(\mu\)</span>. The
normalization <span class="math inline">\(\Phi(\mathbf{0})=1\)</span>
implies <span class="math inline">\(\mu(\mathbb{R}^k)=1\)</span>, so
<span class="math inline">\(\mu\)</span> is a probability measure. Since
<span class="math inline">\(\Phi\)</span> is real-valued, <span
class="math inline">\(\mu\)</span> can be chosen centrally symmetric,
and taking real parts yields <span
class="math display">\[\Phi(\Delta\mathbf{p})
=
\int_{\mathbb{R}^k}\cos(\Delta\mathbf{p}^\top
\boldsymbol{\omega})\,d\mu(\boldsymbol{\omega}),\]</span> which is <span
class="math inline">\(\eqref{eq:Phi-def}\)</span> with <span
class="math inline">\(p=\mu\)</span>. ◻</p>
</div>
<h2 id="variance-analysis-and-convergence">Variance analysis and
convergence</h2>
<p>The Master Theorem describes the mean behavior. With finite <span
class="math inline">\(D\)</span>, <span class="math inline">\(S\)</span>
is a random estimator of its expectation.</p>
<div class="proposition">
<p><strong>Proposition 4</strong> (Second moment and variance of a
block). <em> Assume <span
class="math inline">\(p(\boldsymbol{\omega})\)</span> is centrally
symmetric and let <span
class="math inline">\(u=\Delta\mathbf{p}\cdot\boldsymbol{\omega}\)</span>.
For a fixed block <span class="math inline">\(i\)</span>, define <span
class="math inline">\(A_i=\mathbf{q}_{m,i}^\top
\mathbf{k}_{n,i}\)</span> and <span
class="math inline">\(B_i=\mathbf{q}_{m,i}^\top
J\mathbf{k}_{n,i}\)</span> as in the proof of Theorem <span
class="math inline">\(2\)</span>. Let <span
class="math inline">\(\Phi(\Delta\mathbf{p})=\mathbb{E}[\cos u]\)</span>
and <span
class="math inline">\(\Phi_2(\Delta\mathbf{p})=\mathbb{E}[\cos(2u)]\)</span>.
Then <span class="math display">\[\mathbb{E}[S_i] =
A_i\,\Phi(\Delta\mathbf{p}),\]</span> and the variance satisfies <span
class="math display">\[\mathrm{Var}(S_i)
=
\frac{A_i^2+B_i^2}{2}
+
\frac{A_i^2-B_i^2}{2}\,\Phi_2(\Delta\mathbf{p})
-
A_i^2\,\Phi(\Delta\mathbf{p})^2.\]</span></em></p>
</div>
<div class="proof">
<p><em>Proof.</em> We already derived <span
class="math inline">\(\mathbb{E}[S_i]=A_i\Phi(\Delta\mathbf{p})\)</span>.
For the second moment, write <span class="math inline">\(S_i=A_i\cos u +
B_i\sin u\)</span>. Expanding, <span class="math display">\[S_i^2 =
A_i^2\cos^2 u + B_i^2\sin^2 u + 2A_iB_i\sin u\cos u.\]</span> Taking
expectations and applying Lemma <span
class="math inline">\(1\)</span> gives <span
class="math inline">\(\mathbb{E}[\sin u\cos u]=0\)</span>. Using the
identities <span class="math display">\[\cos^2
u=\frac{1+\cos(2u)}{2},\qquad \sin^2 u=\frac{1-\cos(2u)}{2},\]</span> we
obtain <span class="math display">\[\mathbb{E}[S_i^2]
=
A_i^2\frac{1+\mathbb{E}[\cos(2u)]}{2}
+
B_i^2\frac{1-\mathbb{E}[\cos(2u)]}{2}
=
\frac{A_i^2+B_i^2}{2}+\frac{A_i^2-B_i^2}{2}\,\Phi_2(\Delta\mathbf{p}).\]</span>
Finally, <span
class="math inline">\(\mathrm{Var}(S_i)=\mathbb{E}[S_i^2]-\mathbb{E}[S_i]^2\)</span>
yields the stated expression. ◻</p>
</div>
<p>Because <span class="math inline">\(\boldsymbol{\omega}_i\)</span>
are i.i.d., the random variables <span
class="math inline">\(S_i\)</span> are independent conditioned on fixed
content vectors. Hence, <span
class="math display">\[\mathrm{Var}(S)=\sum_{i=1}^{D}\mathrm{Var}(S_i).\]</span>
In regimes where block magnitudes are comparable (e.g., after
layernorm), the standard deviation grows like <span
class="math inline">\(\sqrt{D}\)</span> while the mean magnitude grows
like <span class="math inline">\(D\)</span>, yielding a typical
<em>relative</em> Monte Carlo error that scales as <span
class="math inline">\(O(D^{-1/2})=O(d^{-1/2})\)</span>.</p>
<h1 id="application-to-rf-rope-design">Application to RF-RoPE
Design</h1>
<p>By Corollary <span class="math inline">\(3\)</span>,
specifying a desired kernel <span class="math inline">\(\Phi\)</span>
determines a spectral distribution <span
class="math inline">\(p(\boldsymbol{\omega})\)</span> (or density when
it exists), from which RF-RoPE samples frequencies. The following
standard examples follow directly from classical kernel/spectral pairs
<span class="citation"
data-cites="rasmussen2006gpml rahimi2007random">(<a
href="#ref-rasmussen2006gpml" role="doc-biblioref">Rasmussen and
Williams 2006</a>; <a href="#ref-rahimi2007random"
role="doc-biblioref">Rahimi and Recht 2007</a>)</span>.</p>
<h4 id="gaussian-rbf-kernel.">Gaussian (RBF) kernel.</h4>
<p><span
class="math display">\[\Phi(\Delta\mathbf{p})=\exp\!\left(-\frac{1}{2\sigma^2}\|\Delta\mathbf{p}\|^2\right),
\qquad
\boldsymbol{\omega}\sim
\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0},\sigma^{-2}I_k).\]</span></p>
<h4 id="cauchy-kernel-1d.">Cauchy kernel (1D).</h4>
<p><span class="math display">\[\Phi(\Delta m)=\left(1+(\Delta
m/b)^2\right)^{-1},
\qquad
\omega \sim \mathrm{Laplace}(0,b^{-1})\ \ (\text{equivalently
}p(\omega)\propto e^{-|\omega|/b}).\]</span></p>
<h4 id="sinc-band-limited-kernel.">Sinc / band-limited kernel.</h4>
<p>For separable bandwidths <span class="math inline">\(W_j\)</span>,
<span
class="math display">\[\Phi(\Delta\mathbf{p})=\prod_{j=1}^{k}\mathrm{sinc}(W_j\Delta
p_j),
\qquad
\omega_j \sim \mathrm{Unif}(-W_j,W_j)\ \text{
independently.}\]</span></p>
<h4 id="matérn-family.">Matérn family.</h4>
<p>Matérn kernels interpolate between rough and smooth locality <span
class="citation" data-cites="matern1960spatial rasmussen2006gpml">(<a
href="#ref-matern1960spatial" role="doc-biblioref">Matérn 1960</a>; <a
href="#ref-rasmussen2006gpml" role="doc-biblioref">Rasmussen and
Williams 2006</a>)</span>. Their spectral densities exhibit
Student-<span class="math inline">\(t\)</span>-like heavy tails,
motivating heavy-tailed sampling schemes for <span
class="math inline">\(\boldsymbol{\omega}\)</span>.</p>
<h1 id="analytical-re-assessment-of-standard-rope">Analytical
Re-Assessment of Standard RoPE</h1>
<p>Standard deterministic RoPE for <span
class="math inline">\(k=1\)</span> uses a fixed geometric frequency list
<span class="citation" data-cites="su2021roformer">(<a
href="#ref-su2021roformer" role="doc-biblioref">Su et al.
2021</a>)</span>. Writing these frequencies as <span
class="math inline">\(\theta_i=B^{-2i/d}\)</span>, the RoPE-modulated
dot product can be expressed in the form <span
class="math display">\[S(m,n)=\sum_{i=1}^{D}\Big[A_i\cos(\Delta
m\,\theta_i)+B_i\sin(\Delta m\,\theta_i)\Big],\]</span> for coefficients
<span class="math inline">\(A_i,B_i\)</span> determined by the content
vectors within each 2D block (cf. <span
class="math inline">\(\eqref{eq:Si-decomp}\)</span>).</p>
<h2 id="asymmetry-and-loss-of-multiplicative-kernel-form">Asymmetry and
loss of multiplicative kernel form</h2>
<p>RF-RoPE relies on symmetric sampling to guarantee <span
class="math inline">\(\mathbb{E}[\sin(\Delta m\,\omega)]=0\)</span> and
hence a clean multiplicative modulation (Theorem <span
class="math inline">\(2\)</span>). In standard RoPE, the
frequency list is strictly positive, so there is no mechanism enforcing
cancellation of the sine term. As a result, standard RoPE does not, in
general, admit a representation of the form <span
class="math display">\[S(m,n)\stackrel{?}{=}(\mathbf{q}_m^\top\mathbf{k}_n)\,\Phi(\Delta
m)\]</span> for a single real-valued scalar kernel <span
class="math inline">\(\Phi\)</span> without additional assumptions on
the content-dependent coefficients.</p>
<h2
id="implied-spectral-density-and-slow-decay-heuristic-continuum-limit">Implied
spectral density and slow decay (heuristic continuum limit)</h2>
<p>A geometric grid <span
class="math inline">\(\theta_i=B^{-2i/d}\)</span> corresponds to an
approximately log-uniform spacing. Treat <span
class="math inline">\(i\)</span> as continuous and solve for <span
class="math inline">\(i(\theta)\)</span>: <span
class="math display">\[\theta = B^{-2i/d}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
i(\theta)= -\frac{d}{2}\log_B\theta.\]</span> Thus, <span
class="math display">\[\left|\frac{di}{d\theta}\right|
=
\frac{d}{2}\cdot \frac{1}{|\ln B|}\cdot \frac{1}{\theta}
\;\propto\; \frac{1}{\theta}.\]</span> Interpreting <span
class="math inline">\(\left|di/d\theta\right|\)</span> as a continuum
density suggests an implied heavy-tailed spectrum <span
class="math inline">\(p(\theta)\propto 1/\theta\)</span> over the
covered band. Heavy tails place substantial mass on low frequencies,
which tends to induce slowly decaying correlations in position space,
consistent with RoPE’s empirically weak inherent locality prior <span
class="citation" data-cites="su2021roformer">(<a
href="#ref-su2021roformer" role="doc-biblioref">Su et al.
2021</a>)</span>.</p>
<div class="remark">
<p><em>Remark 5</em>. The above <span
class="math inline">\(p(\theta)\propto 1/\theta\)</span> argument is a
density-of-states approximation: it describes how densely the
deterministic grid samples log-frequency. RF-RoPE replaces this implicit
bias with an explicit, designer-chosen <span
class="math inline">\(p(\omega)\)</span> tied to a target kernel via
Corollary <span class="math inline">\(3\)</span>.</p>
</div>
<h1
id="beyond-initialization-learned-adaptations-and-shifted-attention">Beyond
Initialization: Learned Adaptations and Shifted Attention</h1>
<p>RF-RoPE controls the <em>initialization</em> kernel. Yet Transformers
learn attention heads that focus on fixed, nonzero offsets (e.g.,
induction heads) <span class="citation"
data-cites="olsson2022incontext">(<a href="#ref-olsson2022incontext"
role="doc-biblioref">Olsson et al. 2022</a>)</span>. We now formalize
why RF-RoPE kernels are necessarily centered and how learning can induce
shifts.</p>
<h2 id="a-locality-constraint-at-initialization">A locality constraint
at initialization</h2>
<div class="theorem">
<p><strong>Theorem 6</strong> (Peak at the origin). <em> Let <span
class="math inline">\(\Phi(\Delta\mathbf{p})=\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{\omega}\sim
p}[\cos(\Delta\mathbf{p}\cdot\boldsymbol{\omega})]\)</span> for any
probability distribution <span class="math inline">\(p\)</span>. Then
<span class="math inline">\(\Phi\)</span> attains its global maximum at
<span class="math inline">\(\Delta\mathbf{p}=\mathbf{0}\)</span>, and
<span class="math inline">\(\Phi(\Delta\mathbf{p})\le 1\)</span> for all
<span class="math inline">\(\Delta\mathbf{p}\)</span>.</em></p>
</div>
<div class="proof">
<p><em>Proof.</em> <span
class="math inline">\(\Phi(\mathbf{0})=\mathbb{E}[\cos 0]=1\)</span>.
For any <span class="math inline">\(\Delta\mathbf{p}\)</span>, <span
class="math inline">\(\cos(\Delta\mathbf{p}\cdot\boldsymbol{\omega})\le
1\)</span> pointwise, hence <span
class="math inline">\(\Phi(\Delta\mathbf{p})=\mathbb{E}[\cos(\Delta\mathbf{p}\cdot\boldsymbol{\omega})]\le
1\)</span>. ◻</p>
</div>
<p>Thus, selecting a different <span
class="math inline">\(p(\boldsymbol{\omega})\)</span> can change the
<em>shape</em> (width, smoothness, tails) of locality, but cannot move
the peak away from <span
class="math inline">\(\Delta\mathbf{p}=\mathbf{0}\)</span> at
initialization.</p>
<h2 id="learned-phase-shifts-from-w_q-and-w_k">Learned phase shifts from
<span class="math inline">\(W_Q\)</span> and <span
class="math inline">\(W_K\)</span></h2>
<p>Consider a single block <span class="math inline">\(i\)</span> and
suppose training makes the query/key projections implement additional
content-dependent rotations within that <span
class="math inline">\(2\)</span>D subspace. Concretely, assume there
exist angles <span class="math inline">\(\phi_{q,i},\phi_{k,i}\)</span>
(possibly head- and block-specific) such that the pre-RoPE projected
vectors satisfy <span
class="math display">\[\tilde{\mathbf{q}}_{m,i}\approx
R(\phi_{q,i})\,\mathbf{v}_{m,i},
\qquad
\tilde{\mathbf{k}}_{n,i}\approx
R(\phi_{k,i})\,\mathbf{v}_{n,i},\]</span> for some base content vectors
<span class="math inline">\(\mathbf{v}_{m,i},\mathbf{v}_{n,i}\)</span>.
Applying RF-RoPE yields <span
class="math display">\[\mathbf{q}&#39;_{m,i}=R(\mathbf{p}_m\cdot\boldsymbol{\omega}_i)\tilde{\mathbf{q}}_{m,i},
\qquad
\mathbf{k}&#39;_{n,i}=R(\mathbf{p}_n\cdot\boldsymbol{\omega}_i)\tilde{\mathbf{k}}_{n,i}.\]</span>
Since rotations commute in 2D, <span
class="math display">\[\mathbf{q}&#39;_{m,i}\approx
R(\mathbf{p}_m\cdot\boldsymbol{\omega}_i+\phi_{q,i})\mathbf{v}_{m,i},
\qquad
\mathbf{k}&#39;_{n,i}\approx
R(\mathbf{p}_n\cdot\boldsymbol{\omega}_i+\phi_{k,i})\mathbf{v}_{n,i}.\]</span>
Therefore, the relative phase entering the dot product is shifted by
<span
class="math display">\[(\mathbf{p}_n-\mathbf{p}_m)\cdot\boldsymbol{\omega}_i
+ (\phi_{k,i}-\phi_{q,i}).\]</span> Let <span
class="math inline">\(\Phi_i:=\phi_{k,i}-\phi_{q,i}\)</span>. Then block
<span class="math inline">\(i\)</span> contributes approximately <span
class="math display">\[S_i(\Delta\mathbf{p})\approx
C_i\cos(\Delta\mathbf{p}\cdot\boldsymbol{\omega}_i+\Phi_i),
\qquad
C_i:=\mathbf{v}_{m,i}^\top\mathbf{v}_{n,i}.\]</span></p>
<h2 id="shifted-local-attention-as-learned-recentering">Shifted-local
attention as learned recentering</h2>
<p>Suppose the training objective encourages attention to peak near
<span class="math inline">\(\Delta\mathbf{p}=-\mathbf{L}\)</span>.
Maximizing <span class="math inline">\(S(-\mathbf{L})\)</span> over
phases pushes <span
class="math display">\[-\mathbf{L}\cdot\boldsymbol{\omega}_i+\Phi_i
\approx 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\Phi_i \approx \mathbf{L}\cdot\boldsymbol{\omega}_i.\]</span>
Substituting gives the shifted form <span
class="math display">\[S(\Delta\mathbf{p})
\approx \sum_{i=1}^{D}
C_i\cos\big((\Delta\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{L})\cdot\boldsymbol{\omega}_i\big),\]</span>
i.e. learning can <em>recenter</em> the effective kernel away from the
origin even though initialization kernels (Theorem <span
class="math inline">\(6\)</span>) must be centered. This
provides a simple mechanistic explanation for how RoPE-based models can
develop fixed-offset heads such as induction heads <span
class="citation" data-cites="olsson2022incontext">(<a
href="#ref-olsson2022incontext" role="doc-biblioref">Olsson et al.
2022</a>)</span>.</p>
<h1 id="conclusion">Conclusion</h1>
<p>RF-RoPE reframes RoPE frequency selection as kernel engineering. By
sampling frequencies from a designed spectral density, RF-RoPE realizes
any desired shift-invariant positive-definite kernel in expectation at
initialization (Theorem <span
class="math inline">\(2\)</span>), with principled
inverse design via Bochner’s theorem (Corollary <span
class="math inline">\(3\)</span>). We also characterized
finite-sample variance (Proposition <span
class="math inline">\(4\)</span>) and clarified why
initialization kernels are necessarily centered (Theorem <span
class="math inline">\(6\)</span>), while learned
projections can induce shifted-local attention via phase shifts.
Together, these results provide a rigorous foundation for designing and
interpreting RoPE-style positional mechanisms.</p>
