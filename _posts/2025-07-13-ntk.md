Neural Tangent Kernel or NTK by {% cite jacot2018 %} is arguably one of the first very deep theoretical insights about neural networks in the mean-field (infinitely wide) regime. While nowadays its importance has been surpassed by the feature learning (muP) regime, it is nevertheless one of the classical papers that are worth fully understanding for any researcher in the deep learning community with theoretical inclinations. However, I have heard from virtually everyone I have talked to that the paper is very hard, if not impossible, to read, which is of course a big pity. To be very honest, my initial attempt at reading the paper was also met with a lot of hiccups. So at some point, I decided to "reinvent the wheel" by proving essentially all the key results of the paper, without reading the paper. This note is the result of my efforts. While there might be some mistakes here and there (which I would be thankful if you report them to me!), I believe the method shown here replicates all the technical results of the NTK paper. The key aspect of these derivations is that they are completely from scratch. I do not assume anything beyond a few basic mathematical facts, namely the law of large numbers, CLT, and a few tricks about the expectation of Gaussians (Stein's lemma).

Towards the end of the document, there is an exposition of my effort to verify or correct an assumption that I had made in my first attempt about the independence of backwards from forwards. These effects manifest themselves in our algebraic framework as "non-diagonal" terms in the backward kernel (meaning interactions between backwards that correspond to different units). These are exactly the terms that we will ignore if we assume independence of forward and backward, which allows us to use the expectation of product rule and get a very simple straightforward solution. The issue that I originally thought existed was that the number of these interactions could be of the same order as they are decaying, meaning that they could contribute significantly to the backward kernel. The calculations, which admittedly went a lot longer than I had hoped for, showed the opposite. Meaning, they proved these corrections are in fact an order of magnitude smaller than the main terms. I still kept these notes for anyone interested. But feel free to ignore them if not.

### Setup: The Infinite-Width (Mean-Field) Regime

We begin by defining the precise mathematical setting for our analysis. We suppose we have two inputs, $x^\alpha$ and $x^\beta$, and assume they have the same norm, i.e., $\|x^\alpha\|^2 = \|x^\beta\|^2 = \|x\|^2$.

We consider a fully-connected neural network with an input layer, $$L-1$$ hidden layers with widths $$d_1,\ldots, d_{L-1}$$, and a scalar output layer $$d_L=1$$, for a total of $$L$$ layers of activations. For an input $x^\alpha$:

- **Layer 0 (Input):** $$h^{0, \alpha} = x^\alpha \in \mathbb{R}^{d_0}$$
- **Hidden Layers ($$1 \leq \ell < L$$):**

\begin{align}
&z^{\ell, \alpha} = W^{\ell-1} h^{\ell-1, \alpha} + b^{\ell-1} && \text{(Pre-activation)} \cr
&h^{\ell-1, \alpha} = \phi(z^{\ell-1, \alpha}) && \text{(Activation)} \cr
&z^{L, \alpha} = W^{L-1} h^{L-1, \alpha} + b^{L-1} && \text{(output, scalar)}
\end{align}

The weights and biases are initialized as i.i.d. zero-mean Gaussian variables with fan-in variance scaling:
$$
\begin{align}
W^{\ell}_{ij} &\sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_w^2 / d_{\ell}) \\
b^{\ell}_i &\sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_b^2)
\end{align}
$$

**Loss:** For simplicity, as specified in the analysis, we define the loss to be the network output itself, evaluated at the input $$x^\beta$$: $$\mathcal{L} = z^{L, \beta}$$. This captures the essential structure of the gradients without loss of generality.

### Notational Conventions

To streamline the derivations, we adopt a more compact and powerful notation. At each layer $$\ell$$, the pre-activations $$(z^{\ell, \alpha}, z^{\ell, \beta})$$ form a bivariate Gaussian vector whose covariance structure is captured by a $$2\times 2$$ matrix $$K_\ell$$. Because we assume equal input norms, this matrix is symmetric:
$$
K_\ell = \begin{pmatrix} 
q_\ell & \rho_\ell \\
\rho_\ell & q_\ell
\end{pmatrix}
$$
where $$q_\ell$$ is the pre-activation variance and $$\rho_\ell$$ is the covariance.

More generally, we define a set of fundamental quantities based on expectations over the activation function $$\phi$$ and its $$n$$-th derivative $$\phi^{(n)}$$:
$$
\begin{align}
q^{(n)}_{\ell+1} &:= \sigma_w^2 \mathbb{E}_{z \sim \mathcal{N}(0, q_{\ell})}\left[(\phi^{(n)}(z))^2\right] \\
\rho^{(n,m)}_{\ell+1} &:= \sigma_w^2 \mathbb{E}_{(z,z')\sim \mathcal{N}(0,K_{\ell})}\left[\phi^{(n)}(z)\phi^{(m)}(z')\right]
\end{align}
$$
This allows us to express the kernel evolution recursively. We are primarily interested in the zeroth and first derivatives, for which we use a simple dot notation as a shorthand:
* **Forward Kernel:** The pre-activation variance and covariance are $$q_{\ell+1} = q^{(0)}_{\ell+1} + \sigma_b^2$$ and $$\rho_{\ell+1} = \rho^{(0,0)}_{\ell+1} + \sigma_b^2$$.
* **Derivative Kernel:** The key quantities for the NTK are $$\dot{q}_{\ell+1} = q^{(1)}_{\ell+1}$$ and $$\dot{\rho}_{\ell+1} = \rho^{(1,1)}_{\ell+1}$$.

**Updated cheatsheet**

| Symbol | Mathematical definition | Description |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| $$K_\ell$$ | $$\begin{pmatrix} q_\ell & \rho_\ell \\ \rho_\ell & q_\ell \end{pmatrix}$$ | **Covariance Matrix**: Layer-$$\ell$$ pre-activation covariance. |
| $$q_\ell, \rho_\ell$$ | $$K_\ell^{\alpha\alpha}, K_\ell^{\alpha\beta}$$ | **Variance** and **Covariance** (scalars). |
| $$\dot{q}_\ell, \dot{\rho}_\ell$$ | $$q^{(1)}_\ell, \rho^{(1,1)}_\ell$$ | Shorthand for first-derivative kernels. |
| $$\dot z^{k \to \ell}$$ | $$(\dot z^{k \to \ell})_i = \sum_{j} \frac{\partial z^{\ell,\alpha}_i}{\partial z^k_j} \frac{\partial z^{L,\beta}}{\partial z^k_j}$$ | Sensitivity flow from layer $$k$$ to $$\ell$$. |
| $$M_1[\dot z^{k \to \ell}]$$ | $$\mathbb E\bigl[(\dot z^{k \to \ell})_{1}\bigr]$$ | First moment (mean) of a sensitivity coordinate. |
| $$M_2[\dot z^{k \to \ell}]$$ | $$\mathbb E\bigl[(\dot z^{k \to \ell})_{1}^{2}\bigr]$$ | Second raw moment of a sensitivity coordinate. |
| Moment recursion | $$M_{1}[\dot z^{k \to \ell}]=\dot \rho_{k}\,M_{1}[\dot z^{k+1 \to \ell}]$$ | $$M_{2}[\dot z^{k \to \ell}]=(\dot \rho_{k})^{2}M_{2}[\dot z^{k+1 \to \ell}]$$ |
| $$\Theta^{(L)}$$ | $$\sum_{k} \eta_0 \rho_{k-1} \prod_{i=k}^{L-1}\dot{\rho}_i$$ | The full Neural Tangent Kernel. |

### Kernel in the Mean-Field Regime

The forward kernel at layer $$\ell$$, $$K_\ell$$, contains the pre-activation variance $$q_\ell$$ and covariance $$\rho_\ell$$. They obey the following recursions, using the compact notation $$\rho_\ell = \rho^{(0,0)}_\ell + \sigma_b^2$$ and $$q_\ell = q^{(0)}_\ell + \sigma_b^2$$:
$$
\begin{align}
q_0 &:= \sigma_w^2 \frac{1}{d_0} \|x\|^2 + \sigma_b^2\cr
\rho_0 &:= \sigma_w^2 \frac{1}{d_0} (x^\alpha)^\top x^\beta + \sigma_b^2\cr
q_{\ell+1} &:= \rho^{(0,0)}_{\ell+1} \text{ with } K_\ell \text{ diagonal} + \sigma_b^2 \cr
\rho_{\ell+1} &:= \rho^{(0,0)}_{\ell+1} + \sigma_b^2
\end{align}
$$
Similarly, the derivative kernels, essential for the NTK, are given by:
$$
\begin{align}
\dot{q}_{\ell+1} &:= q^{(1)}_{\ell+1} = \sigma_w^2 \mathbb{E}_{z \sim \mathcal{N}(0, q_{\ell})} [(\phi'(z))^2] \\
\dot{\rho}_{\ell+1} &:= \rho^{(1,1)}_{\ell+1} = \sigma_w^2 \mathbb{E}_{(z,z')\sim \mathcal{N}(0, K_{\ell})} [\phi'(z)\phi'(z')]
\end{align}
$$

We are interested in how the network representations change under the influence of the gradient of the loss $$\mathcal{L} = z^{L, \beta}$$. Specifically, we analyze the change in the pre-activation $$z^{\ell,\alpha}$$ caused by updating the weights of layer $$k-1$$. The influence of the gradient with respect to weights $$W^{k-1}$$ on the correlation between $$z^{\ell,\alpha}$$ and the loss $$z^{L, \beta}$$ is captured by the term:
$$
\frac{1}{d_\ell} \sum_{i}\left\langle \frac{\partial z^{\ell,\alpha}_i}{\partial W^{k-1}}, \frac{\partial z^{L,\beta}}{\partial W^{k-1}} \right\rangle_{W^{k-1}}
$$
where $$\langle \cdot, \cdot \rangle_{W^{k-1}}$$ denotes the Frobenius inner product. Using the outer product rule for gradients ($$\nabla_W (Wa) = a^\top I$$), this decomposes into:
$$
\langle h^{k-1,\alpha}, h^{k-1,\beta} \rangle \frac{1}{d_\ell} \sum_{i} \left\langle \frac{\partial z^{\ell,\alpha}_i}{\partial z^k}, \frac{\partial z^{L,\beta}}{\partial z^k} \right\rangle_{z^k}
$$
The first term, $$\langle h^{k-1,\alpha}, h^{k-1,\beta} \rangle$$, is the inner product of activations from the forward pass. At large width $$d$$, this term is well-behaved and of order $$O(d)$$. Our focus is therefore on the second term, which involves the Jacobians of the backward passes.

To formalize the analysis of its mean and variance, we define the moments of the sensitivity flow.

**Definition (Sensitivity Flow Moments).** The key quantities for our analysis are the first and second moments of the sensitivity flow $$\dot z^{k \to \ell}$$, whose components are $$(\dot z^{k \to \ell})_i = \sum_{j} \frac{\partial z^{\ell,\alpha}_i}{\partial z^k_j} \frac{\partial z^{L,\beta}}{\partial z^k_j}$$.

$$
\begin{align}
M_1[\dot z^{k \to \ell}] &:= \mathbb{E}\left[(\dot z^{k \to \ell})_{1}\right] &&\text{(first moment)}\\
M_2[\dot z^{k \to \ell}] &:= \mathbb{E}\left[\left((\dot z^{k \to \ell})_{1}\right)^2\right] && \text{(second moment)}\\
\mathrm{Var}[\dot z^{k \to \ell}] &:= M_2[\dot z^{k \to \ell}] - (M_1[\dot z^{k \to \ell}])^2 && \text{(variance)}
\end{align}
$$

Intuitively, we can understand these two terms as the average influence on a representation coordinate at layer $$\ell$$ ($$M_1$$), and its mean square ($$M_2$$).

**Remark.** The first moment $$M_1[\dot z^{k \to \ell}]$$ is defined for a single coordinate ($$i=1$$), but due to symmetry, this is equal to the expectation of the average over all coordinates, $$\frac{1}{d_\ell}\sum_{i}\mathbb{E}[(\dot z^{k \to \ell})_{i}]$$. The reason for keeping the averaging in place in some formulations is to facilitate a clean inductive step, but defining it for a single coordinate is sufficient.

We will derive recursions for $$M_1$$ and $$M_2$$ to determine their scaling with width.

### Recursion for the Mean Influence

A very important and clear fact is the Jacobian of two successive layers:

$$
z^{\ell+1}_i = \sum_{j} W^\ell_{ij} \phi(z^\ell_j) + b_i
\implies
\frac{\partial z^{\ell+1}_i}{\partial z^\ell_j} = W^\ell_{ij}\phi'(z^\ell_j)
$$


Substituting this into the definition of $$M_1[\dot z^{k \to \ell}]$$, we can expand the formula from $$k$$ to the next layer $$k+1$$:
$$
\begin{align}
M_1[\dot z^{k \to \ell}] &:= \mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{j} \frac{\partial z^{\ell,\alpha}_1}{\partial z^k_j} \frac{\partial z^{L,\beta}}{\partial z^k_j} \right]\\
&= \sum_{jsr} \mathbb{E}\left[ \left(\frac{\partial z^{\ell,\alpha}_1}{\partial z^{k+1}_s} \frac{\partial z^{k+1}_s}{\partial z^k_j}\right) \left(\frac{\partial z^{L,\beta}}{\partial z^{k+1}_r} \frac{\partial z^{k+1}_r}{\partial z^{k}_j}\right) \right] \\
&= \sum_{jsr} \mathbb{E}\left[ \left(\frac{\partial z^{\ell,\alpha}_1}{\partial z^{k+1}_s} W^k_{sj} \phi'(z^{k,\alpha}_j)\right) \left(\frac{\partial z^{L,\beta}}{\partial z^{k+1}_r} W^k_{rj} \phi'(z^{k,\beta}_j)\right) \right] \\
&= \sum_{jsr} \mathbb{E}\left[ \frac{\partial z^{\ell,\alpha}_1}{\partial z^{k+1}_s} \frac{\partial z^{L,\beta}}{\partial z^{k+1}_r} \phi'(z^{k,\alpha}_j)\phi'(z^{k,\beta}_j) W^k_{sj} W^k_{rj} \right]
\end{align}
$$

**The independence of weight elements**: This is the first subtle argument in our proof method. In the mean-field limit ($$d \to \infty$$), succeeding layers are asymptotically independent of any single weight element $$W^k_{sj}$$. This allows us to use the product rule for expectation:
$$
M_1[\dot z^{k \to \ell}] = \sum_{jsr} \mathbb{E}\left[ \frac{\partial z^{\ell,\alpha}_1}{\partial z^{k+1}_s} \frac{\partial z^{L,\beta}}{\partial z^{k+1}_r} \phi'(z^{k,\alpha}_j)\phi'(z^{k,\beta}_j) \right] \mathbb{E}[W^k_{sj} W^k_{rj}]
$$

**The pairing of weights**: Since weight elements are i.i.d. and zero-mean, $$\mathbb{E}[W^k_{sj} W^k_{rj}]$$ is non-zero only if $$r=s$$:
$$
\mathbb{E}[W^k_{sj} W^k_{rj}] = \delta_{sr} \frac{\sigma_w^2}{d_k}
$$
Enforcing this pairing by setting $$r = s$$ in our summation:
$$
M_1[\dot z^{k \to \ell}] = \sum_{js} \mathbb{E}\left[ \frac{\partial z^{\ell,\alpha}_1}{\partial z^{k+1}_s} \frac{\partial z^{L,\beta}}{\partial z^{k+1}_s} \phi'(z^{k,\alpha}_j)\phi'(z^{k,\beta}_j) \right] \left(\frac{\sigma_w^2}{d_k}\right)
$$

**CLT**: We rearrange the terms and take the limit of the sum over $$j$$:
$$
M_1[\dot z^{k \to \ell}] = \sum_{s} \mathbb{E}\left[ \frac{\partial z^{\ell,\alpha}_1}{\partial z^{k+1}_s} \frac{\partial z^{L,\beta}}{\partial z^{k+1}_s} \left(\frac{\sigma_w^2}{d_k}\sum_{j} \phi'(z^{k,\alpha}_j)\phi'(z^{k,\beta}_j) \right)\right]
$$
The term in parentheses converges to $$\dot{\rho}_k$$. This reveals the recursion:
$$
M_1[\dot z^{k \to \ell}] = \dot{\rho}_k \sum_{s} \mathbb{E}\left[ \frac{\partial z^{\ell,\alpha}_1}{\partial z^{k+1}_s} \frac{\partial z^{L,\beta}}{\partial z^{k+1}_s} \right] = \dot{\rho}_k M_1[\dot z^{k+1 \to \ell}]
$$
Unrolling this recursion from $$k$$ to $$\ell$$ gives:
$$
M_1[\dot z^{k \to \ell}] = M_1[\dot z^{\ell \to \ell}] \prod_{i=k}^{\ell-1}\dot{\rho}_{i}
$$

### Induction Basis for Mean Influence

We evaluate the basis term $$M_1[\dot z^{\ell \to \ell}] = \mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{j}\frac{\partial z^{\ell,\alpha}_1}{\partial z^\ell_j} \frac{\partial z^{L,\beta}}{\partial z^\ell_j} \right]$$. Since $$\frac{\partial z^{\ell,\alpha}_1}{\partial z^\ell_j} = \delta_{1j}$$, this simplifies to:
$$
M_1[\dot z^{\ell \to \ell}] = \mathbb{E}\left[ \frac{\partial z^{L,\beta}}{\partial z^\ell_1} \right]
$$
We consider two cases:

1.  **$$\ell=L$$ case:** The partial derivative is $$1$$, so $$M_1[\dot z^{L \to L}] = 1$$.

2.  **$$\ell<L$$ case:** We expand the derivative one step:
$$
\begin{align}
M_1[\dot z^{\ell \to \ell}] &= \mathbb{E}\left[ \frac{\partial z^{L,\beta}}{\partial z^\ell_1} \right] = \sum_{j} \mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\partial z^{L,\beta}}{\partial z^{\ell+1}_j} \frac{\partial z^{\ell+1}_j}{\partial z^\ell_1}\right] \\
&= \sum_{j} \mathbb{E} \left[\frac{\partial z^{L,\beta}}{\partial z^{\ell+1}_j} W_{j1}^\ell\phi'(z^{\ell,\beta}_1)\right]
\end{align}
$$
Since $$W_{j1}^\ell$$ is zero-mean and independent of the other terms, the expectation is zero. Thus, $$M_1[\dot z^{\ell \to \ell}] = 0$$ for $$\ell < L$$.

Combining these results gives the full expression for the first moment:
$$
M_1[\dot z^{k \to \ell}] = \begin{cases}
0 & \ell < L\\
\prod_{i=k}^{L-1} \dot{\rho}_i & \ell=L
\end{cases}
$$
The expected contribution to the NTK from layer $$k-1$$ (with learning rate $$\eta_{k-1} = \eta_0/d_{k-1}$$) is:
$$
\mathbb{E} [\text{contribution from } W^{k-1}] = \eta_0 \rho_{k-1} M_1[\dot z^{k \to L}] = \eta_0 \rho_{k-1} \prod_{i=k}^{L-1} \dot{\rho}_i
$$
This applies only to the output layer kernel ($$\ell=L$$). For any other layer, the expected change is zero.

### The Variance of Kernel Shifts

To understand the fluctuations, we evaluate the second moment $$M_2[\dot z^{k \to \ell}]$$. The derivation is similar to the first moment but more involved.
$$
\begin{align}
M_2[\dot z^{k \to \ell}] &= \mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sum_{j} \frac{\partial z^{\ell,\alpha}_1}{\partial z^k_j} \frac{\partial z^{L,\beta}}{\partial z^k_j}\right)^2\right] \\
&= \mathbb{E}\left[\left( \sum_{jrs} \frac{\partial z^{\ell,\alpha}_1}{\partial z^{k+1}_r} W_{rj}^k\phi'(z^{k,\alpha}_j) \frac{\partial z^{L,\beta}}{\partial z^{k+1}_s} W_{sj}^k\phi'(z^{k,\beta}_j) \right)^2\right]
\end{align}
$$
Expanding the square leads to a fourth-order moment of weights, $$\mathbb{E} \left[W_{rj}^k W_{sj}^k W_{r'j'}^k W_{s'j'}^k \right]$$. We apply Wick's theorem for Gaussian variables. The pairings are:
$$
\mathbb{E} [W_{rj}^k W_{sj}^k] \mathbb{E} [W_{r'j'}^k W_{s'j'}^k] + \mathbb{E} [W_{rj}^k W_{r'j'}^k ] \mathbb{E} [ W_{sj}^k W_{s'j'}^k ] + \mathbb{E} [W_{rj}^k W_{s'j'}^k] \mathbb{E} [W_{sj}^k W_{r'j'}^k ]
$$
Counting the non-zero terms reveals that the first pairing is the leading order term, being $$O(d_k)$$ larger than the other two. Keeping only this term, the expectation over the layer $$k$$ variables simplifies to $$(\dot{\rho}_k)^2$$. This gives the recursion:
$$
M_2[\dot z^{k \to \ell}] = (\dot{\rho}_k)^2 \mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sum_{r} \frac{\partial z^{\ell,\alpha}_1}{\partial z^{k+1}_r} \frac{\partial z^{L,\beta}}{\partial z^{k+1}_r}\right)^2\right] = (\dot{\rho}_k)^2 M_2[\dot z^{k+1 \to \ell}]
$$
Unrolling this recursion gives:
$$
M_2[\dot z^{k \to \ell}] = M_2[\dot z^{\ell \to \ell}]\prod_{i=k}^{\ell-1}(\dot{\rho}_i)^2
$$
For the basis term $$M_2[\dot z^{\ell \to \ell}] = \mathbb{E}\left[\left(\frac{\partial z^{L,\beta}}{\partial z^\ell_1}\right)^2\right]$$, we find another recursion:
$$
\begin{align}
M_2[\dot z^{\ell \to \ell}] &= \mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sum_j \frac{\partial z^{L,\beta}}{\partial z^{\ell+1}_j}W_{j1}^\ell\phi'(z_1^{\ell,\beta})\right)^2\right] \\
&= \sum_{jj'} \mathbb{E}\left[ \frac{\partial z^L}{\partial z^{\ell+1}_j} \frac{\partial z^L}{\partial z^{\ell+1}_{j'}} \right] \mathbb{E}[(\phi'(z_1^{\ell,\beta}))^2] \mathbb{E} \left[W_{j1}^\ell W_{j'1}^\ell\right] \\
&= \sum_{j} \mathbb{E}\left[ \left(\frac{\partial z^L}{\partial z^{\ell+1}_j}\right)^2 \right] \mathbb{E}[(\phi'(z_1^{\ell,\beta}))^2] \frac{\sigma_w^2}{d_\ell} \\
&= d_{\ell+1} M_2[\dot z^{\ell+1 \to \ell+1}] \left( \frac{1}{d_{\ell+1}} \frac{\dot{q}_\ell d_\ell}{\sigma_w^2} \frac{\sigma_w^2}{d_\ell} \right) = M_2[\dot z^{\ell+1 \to \ell+1}] \dot{q}_\ell \frac{d_{\ell+1}}{d_\ell}
\end{align}
$$
Note the appearance of the diagonal derivative kernel $$\dot{q}_\ell$$ since the derivative is at a single point $$x^\beta$$. With the base case $$M_2[\dot z^{L \to L}] = 1/d_L = 1$$, this unrolls to:
$$
M_2[\dot z^{\ell \to \ell}] = \frac{1}{d_\ell}\prod_{i=\ell}^{L-1}\dot{q}_i
$$
The full second moment is:
$$
M_2[\dot z^{k \to \ell}] = \frac{1}{d_\ell}\left(\prod_{i=k}^{\ell-1}(\dot{\rho}_i)^2\right) \cdot \left(\prod_{i=\ell}^{L-1}\dot{q}_i\right)
$$
The variance of the contribution from any layer, $$\mathrm{Var}(\text{contrib.})$$, vanishes as $$d \to \infty$$. For $$\ell < L$$, $$M_1 = 0$$, so the variance is the second moment, which is $$O(1/d_\ell)$$. For $$\ell=L$$, the squared mean $$(M_1)^2$$ cancels the leading term of the second moment $$M_2$$, as $$(\dot{\rho}_i)^2$$ in the expression for $$(M_1)^2$$ matches the leading term in $$M_2$$, while the final products differ ($$(\dot{\rho}_{L-1})^2$$ vs $$\dot{q}_{L-1}$$). However, a full analysis shows the variance is dominated by sub-leading terms of order $$O(1/d)$$. In all cases, the variance vanishes and the NTK becomes deterministic.

### Putting It All Together

The total NTK for an $$L$$-layer network, $$\Theta^{(L)}$$, is the sum of the deterministic contributions from all layers. For the change in the output kernel ($$\ell=L$$), we have:
$$
\Theta^{(L)} = \sum_{k} \eta_0 \rho_{k-1}\prod_{i=k}^{L-1}\dot{\rho}_i
$$
Note that we have dropped the expectation, because the variance vanishes in the infinite-width limit. From this formula, we can deduce the famous recursive definition for the NTK. Let $$\Theta^{(\ell)}$$ be the NTK of an $$\ell$$-layer network:
$$
\Theta^{(L)} = \eta_0 \rho_{L-1} + \Theta^{(L-1)} \dot{\rho}_{L-1}
$$
This finishes our NTK derivations and establishes all the key facts in that paper.

### A More Nuanced First Moment Calculation

The previous derivation relied on an independence assumption that, while yielding the correct result, deserves a more rigorous check. The potential issue is that the number of small, off-diagonal (cross-term) interactions could collectively be as large as the diagonal interactions.

### Our Refined Strategy

We proceed by packing all terms dependent on a given weight layer $$W^\ell$$ and carefully computing the expectation using an extended version of Stein's Lemma. This avoids the potentially flawed independence assumption. Using our generalized covariance notation:
$$
M_1[\dot z^{k \to \ell}] = \frac{\rho^{(1,1)}_k}{d_\ell} \sum_{ij} \mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\partial z^{\ell,\alpha}_i}{\partial h^k_j} \frac{\partial z^{L,\beta}}{\partial h^k_j}\right]
$$

We unroll the chain rule formula from $$k$$ to $$k+1$$ with respect to post-activations:
$$
\begin{align}
M_1[\dot z^{k \to \ell}] &= \frac{\rho^{(1,1)}_k}{d_\ell} \sum_{isr} \mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\partial z^{\ell,\alpha}_i}{\partial h^{k+1}_s} \frac{\partial z^{L,\beta}}{\partial h^{k+1}_r}\right]\cdot \underbrace{\sum_{j} \mathbb{E} \left[\phi'(z^{k+1,\alpha}_s)\phi'(z^{k+1,\beta}_r) W^k_{sj} W^k_{rj} \right] }_{T_{rs}:=}
\end{align}
$$

Here we use an extended version of Stein's Lemma to evaluate $$T_{rs}$$. The calculation differs for the diagonal ($$r=s$$) and off-diagonal ($$r \neq s$$) cases.
**Case $$r=s$$:** The leading order term gives $$\mathbb{E}[T_{rr}] = \rho^{(1,1)}_{k+1}$$.
**Case $$r \neq s$$:** The leading order term gives $$\mathbb{E}[T_{rs}] = \frac{1}{d_k} \rho_{k}(\mu^{k+1}_{\phi''})^2$$, where $$\mu^\ell_f := \mathbb{E}_{z \sim \mathcal{N}(0, q_{\ell-1})}[f(z)]$$.

This leads to a coupled system of recursions for a diagonal term accumulator $$D_{k \to \ell}$$ and an off-diagonal one $$S_{k \to \ell}$$.
$$
\begin{align}
D_{k \to \ell} &= \rho^{(1,1)}_{k+1} D_{k+1 \to \ell} + \frac{1}{d_k} \rho_{k} (\mu^{k+1}_{\phi''})^2 S_{k+1 \to \ell} \cr
S_{k \to \ell} &= \ldots
\end{align}
$$

A full derivation shows this can be written as a $$2 \times 2$$ transfer matrix dynamics where the off-diagonal entries are of order $$O(1/d_k)$$. In the infinite-width limit, these terms vanish, and the dynamics are governed purely by the diagonal entries. This rigorously confirms that the simpler derivation was correct at leading order.

**Implication:** Our more detailed analysis validates the original NTK recursion. An interesting observation is that the off-diagonal correction terms are proportional to $$M_\phi = \mathbb{E}[\phi(z)]$$. For activation functions with zero mean (like $$\tanh$$ or centered ReLU), these correction terms are identically zero, not just asymptotically small.

### Concluding Remarks

The key takeaway from our analysis is that there is a drastic difference between the middle layers and the last layer, in that the last layer is the only one that exhibits a first-order change in its kernel during training. Furthermore, we saw that this was a result of asymmetries in the kernel evolution dynamics. The rigorous check of our initial assumptions confirmed that the simpler model was correct at leading order, and the corrections vanish in the infinite-width limit, solidifying the classical NTK result. This progression from detailed first-principles analysis to the celebrated NTK recursion represents a fundamental advance in our theoretical understanding of deep neural networks in the infinite-width limit.
